Skymaps privacy policy

Last updated October 26, 2026.

Skymaps is a weather and stargazing app. This policy is written from the app's code, and the list of services below is every hostname the app contacts.

The short version

Skymaps has no account, no advertising and no tracking, and it does not watch how you use the app. Nothing you do in the app is recorded anywhere, with one exception you choose: if you agree to share them, the Think of a Place games Atlas gets wrong. They are kept against a random access key for your install, never your name, email or an account, and you can delete them from the app.

What stays on your device

Your pinned places, settings, observing log, and what Think of a Place has learned from your games and the places you taught it. They live in the app's storage, are not sent to any server of ours, and are removed when you delete the app. iCloud device backups include them under Apple's terms. The access key for the Skymaps server (described below) is kept in the phone's keychain, on this device only, and is not included in backups.

Versions before 2026.43.4 also kept Home, Work and saved driving destinations on the phone. Updating to 2026.43.4 deleted them, and the app no longer has maps or driving.

The Why screen can shorten the National Weather Service's forecast discussion using Apple's on-device model. That happens on the phone, with text the app already downloaded, and nothing is sent anywhere.

What leaves your device

  • Your location, if you allow it, as a coordinate sent with weather and map requests. It is not stored with anything that identifies you. You can decline and search for places instead.
  • Places you search for, sent to Apple Maps.
  • Trips you plan stay on your device. Their weather is asked for the destination's coordinate from the same weather services as any other place.
  • What's Next picks, from earlier versions only. Versions before 2026.43.4 had a What's Next screen for voting on ideas, which sent the features you picked with your install's access key and a random install number. The screen is gone and the app sends no picks now. Updating to 2026.43.4 deleted the picks and the install number kept on the phone, and the server deleted every pick it had received on October 24, 2026.
  • Think of a Place games Atlas gets wrong, if you agree. Before the first game would be shared, a one-time sheet explains what is shared and asks: Share to Help Atlas or Don't Share. Nothing is sent before you choose, and the Help Atlas Learn switch on the "Which Place Was It?" screen turns it off at any time, including for games still waiting. A shared game holds the place (or, for a place Atlas didn't know, its name from Apple Maps, its country and its position rounded to about 11 km), your answers, whether Atlas won, and how many guesses it used, with your access key and a random install number used only by Think of a Place. Your location, anything you type, and any place name with numbers in it (such as a street address) are never sent. Unsent games wait on the phone for up to 30 days. Once a day at most, the app downloads everyone's combined totals, which carry nothing about you.

No other identifier for you, your device or your install is sent anywhere. Only requests to the Skymaps server carry the install's access key.

The services Skymaps contacts

  • Weather and forecasts: api.weather.gov, Apple WeatherKit, open-meteo.com (including its air quality, archive and ensemble endpoints), www.spc.noaa.gov, services.swpc.noaa.gov.
  • Tides and surf: api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov and www.ndbc.noaa.gov. Their station lists are downloaded whole and the nearest station is found on the phone, so only a station number is sent. The wave forecast comes from api.weather.gov for a point a few kilometers offshore of the place shown.
  • Hazards: earthquake.usgs.gov, volcanoes.usgs.gov, services3.arcgis.com (wildfire).
  • Radar: opengeo.ncep.noaa.gov (radar images), mapservices.weather.noaa.gov (warnings, forecast amounts, fronts, station winds), nowcoast.noaa.gov (satellite, lightning, tropical storms), satepsanone.nesdis.noaa.gov (smoke) and mesonet.agron.iastate.edu (the 12-hour forecast), asked for the area of the map shown.
  • Dark sky: gibs.earthdata.nasa.gov, NASA's night-lights map tiles for the area around the place shown. Drive times to darker skies come from Apple Maps.
  • Ski areas: overpass-api.de (OpenStreetMap), sent a rounded coordinate.
  • Think of a Place: flights.protes.fyi, this app's own server, described below.
  • Sky: celestrak.org.
  • Places: Apple Maps, for place search, the name of the place at a coordinate, parks and beaches near the place shown, and the maps in Think of a Place. When the app isn't in English, Think of a Place also asks Apple Maps for the name of a city Atlas is about to guess, to show it in your language. Directions to a spot open in the Apple Maps or Waze app, which receive only the destination, under their own policies; Skymaps has no navigation of its own.

Each receives only what its request needs: almost always a coordinate or something you typed. A link to a source's own page, such as a weather alert or a volcano observatory, opens in your browser.

The Skymaps server

flights.protes.fyi is a small server run by the developer. Its name is historical: it was built to look up flight schedules, and the app no longer has any flight features. Today it holds Think of a Place's shared answers.

Access keys. To use the server, the app first proves with Apple's App Attest that it is a genuine copy of Skymaps. The server then issues the install a random access key, not derived from you, your phone or any Apple identifier. App Attest tells the server only that the app is genuine. A device that can't use App Attest doesn't use the server, and the app says so.

What it stores:

  • For each install: the public half of its App Attest key, Apple's attestation receipt, a fingerprint of its access key, and when the install registered and was last issued a key.
  • How many times a day each install asked for an access key and downloaded Atlas's totals, kept seven days.

Think of a Place. Games are not kept as games. For each place and question, the server keeps your install's latest answer (one voice per install, for the 150 places it played most recently), plus places you taught (name, country and position rounded to about 11 km, up to 30). Beside them: games won and lost, when your install first and last sent a game, when each answer was last given, games sent per day (kept seven days), and whether the install was left out for sending far more than a person plays or the same answer every time. What it shares with players is totals only: how many installs gave each answer, once at least three have, and taught places once at least three installs taught the same one within about 25 km. From these totals it also works out, and shares, how many installs played each place and each country (once at least three have), and how consistently players answer each question. No key, install number or individual answer is ever shared. The developer sees the same totals, which installs were left out, and can keep a taught name out of them. Your answers are kept until you delete them or the game is retired.

Forget What Atlas Learned (Settings, Tabs) deletes everything tied to your install's games, including any sent under its install number before it had an access key: answers, taught places, game counts and games sent per day. The app retries until the server confirms. After the app is deleted and installed again on the same iPhone, Forget still reaches the games sent with the install's access key, which stays in the phone's keychain. It may not reach games sent from earlier versions under the install number alone, before the install had an access key, because that number is removed with the app. On another iPhone, or once the keychain no longer holds the key (after the phone is erased, for example), the app gets a new access key, and Forget cannot reach games sent with the old one. Just before the delete, your latest answers and taught places are added to plain counts that hold no key, install number or date, so Atlas keeps what it learned without anything pointing back to you. Each count has a ceiling, and installs left out for spam add nothing. So that one install counts once however often it deletes and plays again, the server keeps a scrambled marker for each answer or taught place it added. A marker holds no key, install number, place, position or date, and can only be matched by the server when the same install sends the same answer again; a marker also records an install left out for spam, so deleting does not undo that. Forget does not delete the install's App Attest key and access key fingerprint, which the app needs to keep using the server; the daily counts of key requests and downloads go after seven days as usual.

Not stored: your location, and your IP address beyond ordinary connection handling. Traffic passes through Cloudflare, which handles addresses under its own policy.

Nothing the server holds is shared with or sold to anyone.

Notifications

Weather alerts, sky events, the morning digest and the rain countdown on the Lock Screen are made on your phone. The server sends no notifications and holds no push token.

Children

Skymaps is not directed at children and collects nothing that could identify anyone.

Changes

The date at the top changes with any update, and any change to what leaves your device is called out in the app's What's New screen.
