Skymaps privacy policy

Last updated 9 October 2026.

Skymaps is a weather, maps, flights and stargazing app. This policy is written from the app's code, and the list of services below is every hostname the app contacts.

The short version

Skymaps has no account, no advertising and no tracking, and it does not watch how you use the app. Nothing you do in the app is recorded anywhere, with two exceptions you choose: the features you pick on the What's Next screen, and, if you agree to share them, the Think of a Place games Atlas gets wrong. Both are kept against a random access key for your install, never your name, email or an account, and you can delete both from the app.

What stays on your device

Your pinned places, home and work addresses, saved destinations, settings, followed flights, observing log, and what Think of a Place has learned from your games and the places you taught it. They live in the app's storage, are not sent to any server of ours, and are removed when you delete the app. iCloud device backups include them under Apple's terms.

The Why screen can shorten the National Weather Service's forecast discussion using Apple's on-device model. That happens on the phone, with text the app already downloaded, and nothing is sent anywhere.

What leaves your device

  • Your location, if you allow it, as a coordinate sent with weather and map requests. It is not stored with anything that identifies you. You can decline and search for places instead.
  • Places you search for, sent to Apple Maps.
  • Flight numbers, callsigns or tail numbers you look up or follow, sent to the flight services listed below.
  • Your What's Next picks, when you tap Submit. Pick as many improvements as you like. They are sent with your install's access key and a random install number the app makes for this, and only counts per feature are shown to the developer. No text, name or email is sent. Changing your picks replaces them; Remove My Choices deletes them from the server at once. If the server can't be reached, they wait on your phone until it can.
  • Think of a Place games Atlas gets wrong, if you agree. Before the first game would be shared, a one-time sheet explains what is shared and asks: Share to Help Atlas or Don't Share. Nothing is sent before you choose, and the Help Atlas Learn switch on the "Which Place Was It?" screen turns it off at any time, including for games still waiting. A shared game holds the place (or, for a place Atlas didn't know, its name from Apple Maps, its country and its position rounded to about 11 km), your answers, whether Atlas won, and how many guesses it used, with your access key and a random install number for the game. Your location, anything you type, and any place name with numbers in it (such as a street address) are never sent. Unsent games wait on the phone for up to 30 days. Once a day at most, the app downloads everyone's combined totals, which carry nothing about you.

No other identifier for you, your device or your install is sent anywhere. Only requests to the Skymaps flights server carry the install's access key.

The services Skymaps contacts

  • Weather and forecasts: api.weather.gov, Apple WeatherKit, open-meteo.com (including its air quality, archive and ensemble endpoints), www.spc.noaa.gov, mesonet.agron.iastate.edu, api.rainviewer.com, services.swpc.noaa.gov, aviationweather.gov.
  • Tides and surf: api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov and www.ndbc.noaa.gov. Their station lists are downloaded whole and the nearest station is found on the phone, so only a station number is sent. The wave forecast comes from api.weather.gov for a point a few kilometers offshore of the place shown.
  • Hazards: earthquake.usgs.gov, volcanoes.usgs.gov, services3.arcgis.com (wildfire), nasstatus.faa.gov.
  • Flights: opendata.adsb.fi, api.adsb.lol, api.adsbdb.com, opensky-network.org, and flights.protes.fyi, this app's own server.
  • Sky: celestrak.org.
  • Maps: Apple Maps, and overpass-api.de (OpenStreetMap) for the speed limits, stop signs and speed cameras along a drive, which receives the area around the route. Ride buttons open m.uber.com or ride.lyft.com in your browser, under their policies.

Each receives only what its request needs: almost always a coordinate or something you typed.

The Skymaps flights server

flights.protes.fyi is a small server run by the developer. It exists because the flight schedule provider charges per request, so the server makes those requests and enforces a monthly limit.

Access keys. To use the server, the app first proves with Apple's App Attest that it is a genuine copy of Skymaps. The server then issues the install a random access key, not derived from you, your phone or any Apple identifier. App Attest tells the server only that the app is genuine. A device that can't use App Attest doesn't use the server, and the app says so.

What it stores:

  • For each install: the public half of its App Attest key, Apple's attestation receipt, and a fingerprint of its access key.
  • Flight answers, filed under the flight number only (not your key), for about an hour.
  • Look-ups per key per day, kept for seven days, and requests to the schedule provider per month.
  • Your What's Next picks, against your key.
  • Which key each random install number belongs to, so earlier picks or games count once. This is deleted with them.

It keeps no list of the flights you follow; your phone follows them.

Think of a Place. Games are not kept as games. For each place and question, the server keeps your install's latest answer (one voice per install, for the 150 places it played most recently), plus places you taught (name, country and position rounded to about 11 km, up to 30). Beside them: games won and lost, when your install first and last sent a game, when each answer was last given, games sent per day (kept seven days), and whether the install was left out for sending far more than a person plays or the same answer every time. What it shares with players is totals only: how many installs gave each answer, once at least three have, and taught places once at least three installs taught the same one within about 25 km. No key, install number or individual answer is ever shared. The developer sees the same totals, which installs were left out, and can keep a taught name out of them. Your answers are kept until you delete them or the game is retired.

Forget What Atlas Learned (Settings, Experimental) deletes everything tied to your install: answers, taught places, game counts and daily counters; the app retries until the server confirms. Just before, your latest answers and taught places are added to plain counts that hold no key, install number or date, so Atlas keeps what it learned without anything pointing back to you. Each count has a ceiling, and installs left out for spam add nothing.

Not stored: your location, and your IP address beyond ordinary connection handling. Traffic passes through Cloudflare, which handles addresses under its own policy.

Backups. The database is copied nightly to private storage only the developer can reach, and each copy is deleted after 14 days. Anything you delete is gone from every copy within 14 days.

Nothing the server holds is shared with or sold to anyone.

Notifications

Weather alerts, flight changes, sky events and the morning digest are scheduled on your phone. The server sends no notifications and holds no push token.

Children

Skymaps is not directed at children and collects nothing that could identify anyone.

Changes

The date at the top changes with any update, and any change to what leaves your device is called out in the app's What's New screen.
